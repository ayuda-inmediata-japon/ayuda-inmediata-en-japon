🌐 English Help Guide in Japan

Practical information for English-speaking tourists who need help from arrival at the airport to transport, hotels, health care, police, emergencies and daily problems.

🚨 Emergency numbers in Japan

Use these numbers only for urgent situations.

110 Police
119 Ambulance / Fire
118 Coast Guard
🆘 I need immediate help

Move to a safe place, ask nearby staff for help and show one of these messages.

I need help.

助けてください。

Tasukete kudasai.

Please call the police.

警察を呼んでください。

Keisatsu o yonde kudasai.

Please call an ambulance.

救急車を呼んでください。

Kyūkyūsha o yonde kudasai.

I do not speak Japanese.

日本語が話せません。

Nihongo ga hanasemasen.

✈️ Arriving at the airport
  • Follow signs for Immigration, Baggage Claim and Customs.
  • Keep your passport and arrival information ready.
  • Check the baggage carousel number for your flight.
  • Before leaving, prepare internet access, transport and your hotel address.
  • Use an official airport counter if you need help.

Where is immigration?

入国審査はどこですか？

Nyūkoku shinsa wa doko desu ka?

Where can I collect my baggage?

荷物受取所はどこですか？

Nimotsu uketorijo wa doko desu ka?

🧳 Lost or damaged baggage
  • Do not leave the baggage area before reporting the problem.
  • Go to the airline baggage counter.
  • Show your baggage claim tag and boarding pass.
  • Give the address and phone number of your accommodation.

My baggage has not arrived.

荷物が届いていません。

Nimotsu ga todoite imasen.

My suitcase is damaged.

スーツケースが壊れています。

Sūtsukēsu ga kowarete imasu.

📶 Internet, SIM cards and charging
  • Airports usually have SIM, eSIM and pocket Wi-Fi counters.
  • Many stations, hotels and convenience stores offer Wi-Fi.
  • Keep a portable battery and charging cable with you.
  • Save important addresses and maps for offline use.

Is there free Wi-Fi here?

無料Wi-Fiはありますか？

Muryō waifai wa arimasu ka?

Where can I charge my phone?

携帯電話を充電できる場所はどこですか？

Keitai denwa o jūden dekiru basho wa doko desu ka?

🚕 Taxis and showing an address
  • Use official taxi stands or trusted taxi applications.
  • Taxi doors often open and close automatically.
  • Show the complete destination in Japanese.
  • Keep the hotel phone number available.
  • Confirm accepted payment methods before a long trip.

Please take me to this address.

この住所までお願いします。

Kono jūsho made onegaishimasu.

How much will it cost approximately?

だいたいいくらかかりますか？

Daitai ikura kakarimasu ka?

🚆 Trains, metro and buses
  • Check the line name, platform number and final destination.
  • Use Google Maps or NAVITIME to confirm transfers.
  • Keep your ticket or IC card until you exit.
  • For buses, boarding and payment rules vary by city.
  • Check the time of the last train before going out at night.

Which platform should I use?

何番ホームですか？

Nanban hōmu desu ka?

Does this train go to Kyoto?

この電車は京都に行きますか？

Kono densha wa Kyōto ni ikimasu ka?

I took the wrong train.

電車を間違えました。

Densha o machigaemashita.

🌙 Missed the last train
  • Check whether a late bus is still running.
  • Ask station staff about the first train the next morning.
  • Consider a taxi, business hotel, capsule hotel or internet café.
  • Do not sleep in unsafe or prohibited areas.

I missed the last train.

終電に乗り遅れました。

Shūden ni noriokuremashita.

When is the first train tomorrow?

明日の始発は何時ですか？

Ashita no shihatsu wa nanji desu ka?

🏨 Hotels and accommodation
  • Keep your booking name, confirmation number and hotel address.
  • Contact the hotel if you will arrive late.
  • Some hotels require your passport at check-in.
  • Ask reception for help with taxis, hospitals or lost property.

I have a reservation.

予約があります。

Yoyaku ga arimasu.

I will arrive late.

到着が遅くなります。

Tōchaku ga osoku narimasu.

I lost my room key.

部屋の鍵をなくしました。

Heya no kagi o nakushimashita.

🍜 Restaurants and food allergies
  • Show written information about serious allergies.
  • Ask about ingredients before ordering.
  • Some restaurants use ticket machines near the entrance.
  • Not every restaurant can guarantee an allergy-free meal.

I have a serious food allergy.

重い食物アレルギーがあります。

Omoi shokumotsu arerugī ga arimasu.

Does this contain eggs?

これは卵が入っていますか？

Kore wa tamago ga haitte imasu ka?

I do not eat meat.

肉は食べません。

Niku wa tabemasen.

🏥 Hospital, clinic and pharmacy
  • Call 119 for a serious emergency.
  • For non-urgent problems, search for a clinic or hospital nearby.
  • Bring your passport, insurance information and medication list.
  • Some medical institutions may not offer English support.
  • Travel insurance is strongly recommended.

I feel sick.

具合が悪いです。

Guai ga warui desu.

I need an English-speaking doctor.

英語を話せる医師が必要です。

Eigo o hanaseru ishi ga hitsuyō desu.

I am allergic to this medicine.

この薬にアレルギーがあります。

Kono kusuri ni arerugī ga arimasu.

🚓 Police, theft and lost property
  • Call 110 for an urgent police emergency.
  • For lost property, go to a police box called Kōban (交番).
  • Report a lost passport to the police and contact your embassy.
  • Keep any report number or document provided by the police.

My wallet was stolen.

財布を盗まれました。

Saifu o nusumaremashita.

I lost my passport.

パスポートをなくしました。

Pasupōto o nakushimashita.

Where is the nearest police box?

一番近い交番はどこですか？

Ichiban chikai kōban wa doko desu ka?

💴 Cash, ATMs and card problems
  • Keep some cash because not every place accepts cards.
  • Convenience store and Japan Post ATMs may accept international cards.
  • Check fees and withdrawal limits before confirming.
  • Contact your bank immediately if your card is lost or retained.

My card does not work.

カードが使えません。

Kādo ga tsukaemasen.

The ATM kept my card.

ATMにカードが取り込まれました。

ATM ni kādo ga torikomaremashita.

I was charged twice.

二重に請求されました。

Nijū ni seikyū saremashita.

🛍️ Shopping and consumer problems
  • Keep receipts and screenshots of the transaction.
  • Ask for an explanation before signing or paying.
  • Do not provide passport or card details to unknown callers.
  • Use official tourist-consumer support for serious disputes.

I would like a receipt.

領収書をお願いします。

Ryōshūsho o onegaishimasu.

I think I was overcharged.

料金が高すぎると思います。

Ryōkin ga takasugiru to omoimasu.

🌏 Earthquakes, typhoons and evacuation
  • During an earthquake, protect your head and stay away from glass.
  • Do not rush outside while objects are falling.
  • Follow instructions from staff, local authorities and official alerts.
  • Move to higher ground immediately after a tsunami warning.
  • Use the Safety Tips app and official Japan Safe Travel information.

Where is the evacuation shelter?

避難所はどこですか？

Hinanjo wa doko desu ka?

Is it safe to go outside?

外に出ても安全ですか？

Soto ni dete mo anzen desu ka?

👨‍👩‍👧 Lost child or separated traveler
  • Tell station, airport, hotel or attraction staff immediately.
  • Show a recent photo and describe clothing.
  • Agree on a meeting point before visiting crowded areas.
  • Call police if the person is vulnerable or in danger.

I cannot find my child.

子どもが見つかりません。

Kodomo ga mitsukarimasen.

We became separated.

一緒にいた人とはぐれました。

Issho ni ita hito to haguremashita.

🗺️ Lost or unable to find the hotel
  • Open your saved hotel address in Japanese.
  • Ask station staff, a Kōban or a convenience store employee.
  • Call the hotel and ask staff to explain the route.
  • Do not follow unknown people to isolated places.

I am lost.

道に迷いました。

Michi ni mayoimashita.

Can you show me how to get to this hotel?

このホテルへの行き方を教えてください。

Kono hoteru e no ikikata o oshiete kudasai.

📱 Useful applications and official help
  • Google Maps: routes, stations and nearby places.
  • NAVITIME Japan Travel: trains and transfers.
  • Google Translate: text, voice and camera translation.
  • DeepL: useful written translation.
  • Safety Tips: disaster and emergency alerts.
  • Japan Safe Travel: official safety information.
☎️ Japan Visitor Hotline 🚨 Official safety information
Japan Visitor Hotline: 050-3816-2787. Available 24 hours a day, every day, for tourist information and assistance during accidents or emergencies.
⚠️ Important safety advice
  • Keep passport information and hotel details available.
  • Save emergency contacts and travel-insurance information.
  • Do not give money or personal information to unknown callers.
  • Use official counters, police, hospitals and recognized transport services.
  • For a serious emergency, call 110 or 119 immediately.
This guide provides general orientation. It does not replace police, medical, immigration, legal or emergency services.
